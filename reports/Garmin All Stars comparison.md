# Pulse can beat Garmin's own paid face

The face in the screenshot is **"Garmin All Stars – fēnix 9 Chronos"**, a closed-source $4.99 Connect IQ face published by Garmin itself. It is part of the "All Stars" series of paid remasters of stock watch faces, and its six labelled slots (Top Left, Top Right, Left, Right, Bottom Left, Lower Center) sit on a face that switches between Digital, Analog and Hybrid styles ([Store API](https://apps.garmin.com/api/appsLibraryExternalServices/api/asw/apps/f22c1ef4-7c51-4943-981f-fd181801f2fc)). It is rated **4.4 from 153 reviews**. Users praise its polish. Their complaints are what matter most for Pulse: progress arcs that don't track their values, a thin data catalog for the price, tedious phone-only configuration, a dim always-on display (AOD), lag, and an October 2026 release that removed settings ([Reviews API](https://apps.garmin.com/api/appsLibraryExternalServices/api/asw/apps/f22c1ef4-7c51-4943-981f-fd181801f2fc/reviews?startPageIndex=0&pageSize=30)). Pulse, the user's analog FR965 face with four gauges that move out of the hands' way, already avoids the top complaint, because its arcs are driven by real values. Its gaps are also cheap to close. The best value-to-effort moves are all small edits inside `Fields.mc`, `PantherView.mc` and `FieldMenu.mc`, plus two medium features (long-press to open the native glance, and complication subscriptions) that use `Toybox.Complications` APIs Pulse already imports. Building a full Digital/Hybrid mode or six fixed slots is the expensive path, and it would also dilute Pulse's one distinctive idea.

## All Stars is Garmin re-selling its stock faces

The series dates from about **August 2024**. By October 2026 it had about **27 entries**, nearly all priced at $4.99, including a Forerunner 965 face ([Store keyword search](https://apps.garmin.com/api/appsLibraryExternalServices/api/asw/apps/keywords?keywords=Garmin%20All%20Stars&startPageIndex=0&pageSize=30)). Garmin describes the series as faces where "we remastered the default watch faces built in to our watches" ([epix Pro listing](https://apps.garmin.com/api/appsLibraryExternalServices/api/asw/apps/3544ce6b-4a97-4dab-965b-87ed79ad61df)). Forum threads call this a paywall for faces that used to ship free ([Garmin forum](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/connect-iq-store-ios/380091/new-garmin-all-stars-watch-faces/1812714)). Chronos was first approved on 2026-08-25, its latest version is 2026.9.2.4, and it has 1,000+ downloads. It declares the Positioning, ComplicationSubscriber, SensorHistory and UserProfile permissions. No source code exists publicly ([Store API](https://apps.garmin.com/api/appsLibraryExternalServices/api/asw/apps/f22c1ef4-7c51-4943-981f-fd181801f2fc)).

In the marketing screenshot the slots show the following. **Top Left** is a segmented step arc reading "15000". **Top Right** is a date subdial ("01 AUG") inside an S M T W T F S weekday ring with today highlighted. **Left** is sunrise "7:50". **Right** is battery, shown as an icon in Digital and Hybrid and as a "100%" subdial in Analog. **Bottom Left** is an "88" gauge arc that looks like Body Battery. **Lower Center** is weather, "32°" with "28°|33°" low|high. The digital time is stacked hours over minutes with seconds below, and Hybrid lays skeleton hands over the digits ([Screenshot 1](https://apps.garmin.com/api/appsLibraryExternalServices/api/screenshots/c7395b18-664c-4579-ad79-21392f57ec1d)). The listing advertises **10 digital font sets, 10 hand styles, gradient accents, "micro-color" control and dynamic font scaling**. It also lists the data that faces cannot read: Training Readiness, Training Status, Acute Load, Hill Score, Endurance Score, Active Calories, HRV Status, Tide, No Fly Time and Surface Interval. The settings screenshot shows a "Time Style" enum and per-slot "Data" lists in the Connect IQ phone app ([Screenshot 4](https://apps.garmin.com/api/appsLibraryExternalServices/api/screenshots/e1ffaefa-1c50-4a62-bcde-b562893c9af8)). The full list of options for each slot is not published.

The permissions suggest that All Stars gets weather, sunrise, Body Battery and recovery through `Toybox.Complications`, the same path Pulse uses (see [ADR 0001](../pulse/docs/adr/0001-complications-first-for-field-data.md)). There is no hidden Garmin-only API to envy. Garmin's own face works under the same Connect IQ data limits as Pulse.

## Users punish fake arcs, thin data and phone-only settings

The complaints fall into five clusters, and each one maps onto a decision Pulse has to make.

**Arcs that lie.** Reviewers wrote that the "progress bar for 2h recovery looks exactly the same as 30h", that "the bottom arch never does anything. It's always full", and that "most data did not harmonise with the data progress bars" ([Reviews API](https://apps.garmin.com/api/appsLibraryExternalServices/api/asw/apps/f22c1ef4-7c51-4943-981f-fd181801f2fc/reviews?startPageIndex=0&pageSize=30)). Pulse already does this right. `Fields.read` returns a real 0..1 progress value for battery, steps/goal, Body Battery, Stress and the move bar ([Fields.mc:66-82, 100-105](../pulse/ciq/source/Fields.mc)). `drawGauge` sweeps the arc in proportion to that value, and fields with no progress value get a plain inner ring instead of a decorative fill ([PantherView.mc:146-163](../pulse/ciq/source/PantherView.mc)). Pulse should keep "no arc without a real value" as an invariant and say so on the store listing.

**Data catalog.** Reviewers wanted weekly run and bike distance, calendar, timer, wind, precipitation and Training Readiness ([Reviews API](https://apps.garmin.com/api/appsLibraryExternalServices/api/asw/apps/f22c1ef4-7c51-4943-981f-fd181801f2fc/reviews?startPageIndex=0&pageSize=30)). Several of these exist as complication types available on FR965: WEEKLY_RUN_DISTANCE (19), WEEKLY_BIKE_DISTANCE (20), CALENDAR_EVENTS (12), NOTIFICATION_COUNT (17), PULSE_OX (35), RESPIRATION_RATE (36), INTENSITY_MINUTES (5), SUNSET (14) and HIGH_LOW_TEMPERATURE (39) ([Toybox.Complications](https://developer.garmin.com/connect-iq/api-docs/Toybox/Complications.html)). Training Readiness has no Connect IQ path at all, and SLEEP_SCORE (42) needs API 6.0.2, which rules it out on the FR965 at API 5.2 ([Toybox.Complications](https://developer.garmin.com/connect-iq/api-docs/Toybox/Complications.html), [compatible devices](https://developer.garmin.com/connect-iq/compatible-devices/)).

**Configuration friction.** Reviewers called the phone-side loop "tedious" and asked to change everything on the watch. Then v2026.9.2.4 shipped with no settings at all, which drew a run of 1★ reviews ([Reviews API](https://apps.garmin.com/api/appsLibraryExternalServices/api/asw/apps/f22c1ef4-7c51-4943-981f-fd181801f2fc/reviews?startPageIndex=0&pageSize=30)). The latest What's New for Chronos is "You can now adjust the accent color on your watch" ([Store API](https://apps.garmin.com/api/appsLibraryExternalServices/api/asw/apps/f22c1ef4-7c51-4943-981f-fd181801f2fc)). Pulse's on-watch `FieldMenu` covers only the four fields ([FieldMenu.mc:16-57](../pulse/ciq/source/FieldMenu.mc)), even though accent and ring style feed the same `loadSettings()` path ([PantherView.mc:38-45](../pulse/ciq/source/PantherView.mc)).

**AOD and lag.** One reviewer called the AOD "terribly dim and basically unusable". Others call the face "laggy" next to the stock Chronos, and a Forerunner 170 All Stars changelog lists a fix for lag on the FR970 ([Reviews API](https://apps.garmin.com/api/appsLibraryExternalServices/api/asw/apps/f22c1ef4-7c51-4943-981f-fd181801f2fc/reviews?startPageIndex=0&pageSize=30), [FR170 listing](https://apps.garmin.com/apps/6a53acfa-53c1-4511-aba3-1316aa4cfe9b)). Pulse's AOD draws 1 px outline hands in a muted 0x84718f with anti-aliasing off ([PantherView.mc:53-59, 209-214](../pulse/ciq/source/PantherView.mc)). That is burn-in safe, but it is the same "too dim" risk reviewers flagged on Chronos, so it needs checking on a real watch. On cost, Pulse already caches field values per minute and the chapter ring in a buffered bitmap ([PantherView.mc:61-67, 88-100](../pulse/ciq/source/PantherView.mc)), which is the right shape.

**Small toggles.** Reviewers asked for separate seconds-hand and seconds-digit toggles, 24 h time in Hybrid, battery % beside the icon, and hands that disappear on long-press ([Reviews API](https://apps.garmin.com/api/appsLibraryExternalServices/api/asw/apps/f22c1ef4-7c51-4943-981f-fd181801f2fc/reviews?startPageIndex=0&pageSize=30)). Pulse always draws its seconds hand in active mode ([PantherView.mc:77](../pulse/ciq/source/PantherView.mc)) and has no setting to hide it.

## Nine improvements ranked by value against effort

Effort estimates are judgments from reading the code, not measured figures. "Low" means a self-contained edit of roughly 1–20 lines. All the APIs listed are available at or below FR965's API level 5.2. The manifest's `minApiLevel="4.2.1"` and existing `ComplicationSubscriber` permission already cover all of them ([manifest.xml:3-10](../pulse/ciq/manifest.xml)).

Items 1–5 together are an afternoon's work and close most of the visible gap with the screenshot. Item 1 should use `Toybox.Weather` rather than the HIGH_LOW_TEMPERATURE complication. Pulse's `complication()` helper drops any non-numeric value ([Fields.mc:43](../pulse/ciq/source/Fields.mc)), and a hi/lo pair is not obviously a single number. `Weather` needs only an import, and its fields can each be null ([Toybox.Weather](https://developer.garmin.com/connect-iq/api-docs/Toybox/Weather.html)). For item 6, the weekly-distance complication is the most direct answer to the "not worth the price" reviews. For item 8, Pulse's moving gauges are not a problem, because `circles` already holds each gauge's live centre and radius, so the hit-test is `dx*dx+dy*dy < r*r`. The caveats are that `onPress` fires on touch-and-hold only, that it was reported not firing for the FR955 in the simulator, and that `exitTo` behaved differently for native complications on some devices ([forum: complication samples](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/discussion/315443/here-are-a-few-complication-samples/1588917)). It should therefore be a bonus feature, never something the face depends on. Item 9 has a constraint: `subscribeToUpdates` throws `OperationNotAllowedException` past an undocumented subscription limit ([Complications](https://developer.garmin.com/connect-iq/api-docs/Toybox/Complications.html)). Subscribe only to the four chosen fields, and call `unsubscribeFromAllUpdates()` in `loadSettings()`.

Two hardening items cost minutes and belong in the same pass. `Fields.zone()` casts `customOffset` and `useCustomZone` without null checks ([Fields.mc:112-113](../pulse/ciq/source/Fields.mc)). `FieldMenu.fieldAt` falls back to Heart rate where the view falls back to `DEFAULTS[i]` ([FieldMenu.mc:12-13](../pulse/ciq/source/FieldMenu.mc) vs [PantherView.mc:38-39](../pulse/ciq/source/PantherView.mc)).

## Digital, Hybrid and six fixed slots cost the most and fit Pulse least

A full Time Style switch would need a digital renderer with font sets, a hybrid composition, and a settings enum read in `onSettingsChanged()`. Connect IQ has no dedicated API for any of this, so it is a hand-built strategy swap ([forum: settings on WatchFace](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/discussion/406273/settings-on-watchface-not-working/1910203)). Pulse also hard-codes 454×454 geometry in `PantherView.mc:10` and `Layout.mc:5-12`, and its layout engine exists to move gauges away from the hands ([Layout.mc:58-118](../pulse/ciq/source/Layout.mc)). Fixed All Stars-style slots would undo that engine. A cheaper path gives most of the benefit: add a **"Local time" field** that renders `hhmm()` ([Fields.mc:49](../pulse/ciq/source/Fields.mc)) inside a gauge, honouring 12/24 h the way the Zone field already does. That gives Pulse a hybrid-style digital readout with 24 h support, which Chronos reviewers say is missing in its Hybrid mode, for about ten lines, and it doesn't touch the layout engine. A **weekday ring** around the date is possible with `dc.drawRadialText` (4.2.1, vector font) ([Dc](https://developer.garmin.com/connect-iq/api-docs/Toybox/Graphics/Dc.html)). Inside a gauge that is 44–54 px in radius and moves every few minutes, though, the text would be too small to read, so it is not worth the effort. Going from four slots to six would crowd the 8-position ring that [CONTEXT.md](../pulse/CONTEXT.md) deliberately limits to four, and it is not recommended.

The AOD deserves a measured change rather than a redesign. Pulse already follows the AMOLED rules of about 10% of pixels lit and no pixel lit for more than 3 minutes, using a four-step pixel shift ([PantherView.mc:14, 210](../pulse/ciq/source/PantherView.mc); [forum: CIQ 3.1 notes](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/discussion/192673/how-to-enable-an-always-on-mode-for-venu-amoled)). Given the "terribly dim" reviews of Chronos, the cheap experiment is a brighter outline colour or a 2 px pen width for the AOD hands. Each candidate should be checked with the simulator's burn-in checker before anything else is added to the AOD. Segment34 adds data fields to its AOD only as an opt-in setting ([Segment34mkII](https://github.com/ludw/Segment34mkII)), and Pulse should do the same if it ever shows data in AOD.

## Conclusion

The comparison reverses the obvious reading of the screenshot. All Stars is not ahead of Pulse on technique. It runs on the same Complications data under the same Connect IQ limits, and its lowest ratings come from the shortcuts Pulse has avoided: decorative arcs, config only on the phone, and a settings regression. Pulse's opportunity is to match the breadth of Chronos's data (hi/lo weather, sunset, month, weekly distance) and its on-watch customization at low cost, while keeping the honest arcs and the hands-dodging layout that a fixed-slot face can't offer. Before the store submission now being prepared in `pulse/store/`, the highest-leverage step is not a feature. It is checking on a real FR965 that every complication-backed field returns data and that the settings appear in the Connect IQ app. Chronos's October release shows that one broken settings release wipes out goodwill faster than any missing feature. The store listing should then copy what Garmin did well and show screenshots of each mode, the AOD, and the settings page.
